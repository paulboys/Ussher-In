## Interlinear

*⟵ The opening lines of this page complete the final sentence of p0052 (seg_p0052_s0002), translated there. This page's own text begins below at l0010.*

**LA**  In chronico<sup id="fnref-p0053-p"><a href="#fn-seg_p0053_fn_001">p</a></sup> quoque Glastoniensi anno MCCLIX. exarato, scriptum legimus: "Anno ab Incarnatione LXIII. discipuli sanctorum Philippi et Jacobi apostolorum venerunt in Britanniam: a quibus primum oratorium in insula Avalloniæ, in honore S. Mariæ fuit constructum, in anno post assumptionem ejusdem quinto decimo." Et in ali0<sup id="fnref-p0053-q"><a href="#fn-seg_p0053_fn_004">q</a></sup>, a monacho cœnobii S. Augustini Cantuariensis anno MCCCCVI. conscripto: "Rex Yna fuit primus fundator monasterii Glastoniensis. Monachi tamen ibidem aliam prætendunt fundationem a rege Arvirago (filio Kimbelini regis Britonum, in cujus tempore Christus Jesus de Maria virgine natus fuit) et quod ab Arthuro rege dotatum fuit pluribus possessionibus." Item in libro de prærogativis et dignitatibus ordinem monasticum concernentibus, qui a Thoma Walsinghamio scriptus putatur: "S. Philippus apostolus Jesu Christi misit in Britanniam duodecim prædicatores, quorum primus et præcipuus fuit Joseph ab Arimathia, qui Christum sepelivit. Qui Glasconiam venientes, acceperunt possessionem hidarum duodecim, ab Arvirago nobili rege Britanniæ, adhuc paganicis erroribus involuto. Hi per Gabrielis archangeli admonitionem, ecclesiam in honore sanctæ Dei genetricis Mariæ construxerunt anno post passionem Domini tricesimo primo."

**EN**  In the Glastonbury chronicle<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0053_fn_001">p</a></sup> too, written out in the year 1259, I read the following: "In the year 63 from the Incarnation, disciples of the holy apostles Philip and James came into Britain, by whom the first oratory was built on the island of Avalon in honour of St Mary, in the fifteenth year after her assumption." And in another<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0053_fn_004">q</a></sup>, composed in the year 1406 by a monk of the abbey of St Augustine of Canterbury: "King Ine was the first founder of the monastery of Glastonbury. The monks there, however, allege another foundation, by King Arviragus (son of Cymbeline, king of the Britons, in whose time Christ Jesus was born of the virgin Mary), and claim that it was endowed by King Arthur with many possessions." Likewise in the book "On the Prerogatives and Dignities Concerning the Monastic Order", which is thought to have been written by Thomas Walsingham: "St Philip, apostle of Jesus Christ, sent into Britain twelve preachers, of whom the first and chief was Joseph of Arimathea, who buried Christ. Coming to Glastonbury, they received possession of twelve hides from Arviragus, a noble king of Britain, still entangled in pagan errors. At the admonition of the archangel Gabriel they built a church in honour of Mary, the holy mother of God, in the thirty-first year after the Lord's passion."

**LA**  Johannes Tinmuthensis, ex Glastoniensis monasterii libris, ita rem narrat: "Ex<sup id="fnref-p0053-r"><a href="#fn-seg_p0053_fn_002">r</a></sup> quo apostoli in regiones diversas erant divisi ad prædicandum verbum vitæ, sanctus Philippus apostolus regionem Franciæ cum suis discipulis sortitus est: de quibus misit in Britanniam duodecim (quorum primus erat Joseph ab Arimathia, qui et Dominum sepelivit) anno ab incarnatione Domini sexagesimo tertio, et ab assumptione sanctæ Mariæ quinto decimo : quibus duodecim hidæ ab Arvirago paganicis erroribus decepto concessæ erant. Qui ibidem Deo servientes, per Gabrielis archangeli admonitionem; ecclesiam in honore sanctæ Dei genetricis Mariæ construxerant. Cumque fidem Christi constanter prædicarent, rex barbarus cum gente sua tam nova audiens et inconsueta, omnino prædicationi eorum consentire renuebat, nec paternas traditiones commutare volebat: quia tamen de longe venerant, ad petitionem eorum quandam insulam sylvis paludibusque circumdatam ad inhabitandum concessit. Erat enim portio illa duodecim hidarum, et quandam capellam (ut præmittitur) ex virgis torquatis construxerunt anno post passionem Domini XXXI.” Hæc Johannes<sup id="fnref-p0053-s"><a href="#fn-seg_p0054_fn_001">s</a></sup> Tinmuthensis, in sanctilogio Britanniæ, et ejus exscriptor Johannes Capgravius in nova legenda Angliæ, sed prudenter præmisso hoc prologo. “ Quæ inferius digesta sunt, apud Glastoniam ex libris monasterii illius excerpsi: quæ si veritatem sapiant, lectoris arbitrio relinquo.” Et hoc ad extremum subjecto epilogo. “ Ego vero in præscriptis et dubiis, et in sequentibus nonnullis, authoritatem discutiendi et definiendi mihi non præsumo: sed tanquam relator simplex, quæ in diversis libris et locis cum laboribus et difficultate sedulus indagator reperire potui, ardua peritis ventilanda relinquens, sine invidia communico.”

**EN**  John of Tynemouth, drawing on the books of the monastery of Glastonbury, relates the matter thus: "After<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0053_fn_002">r</a></sup> the apostles had been dispersed into diverse regions to preach the word of life, the holy apostle Philip was allotted the region of France together with his disciples, from whom he sent twelve into Britain (the first of whom was Joseph of Arimathea, who also buried the Lord), in the sixty-third year from the Lord's incarnation and the fifteenth from the assumption of St Mary; and to these, twelve hides were granted by Arviragus, who was still deceived by pagan errors. Serving God there, at the admonition of the archangel Gabriel they built a church in honour of Mary, the holy mother of God. And when they steadfastly preached the faith of Christ, the barbarian king and his people, hearing things so new and unaccustomed, altogether refused to consent to their preaching, nor was he willing to change his ancestral traditions; yet because they had come from afar, at their petition he granted them a certain island, surrounded by woods and marshes, to dwell in. For that portion amounted to twelve hides, and they built a certain chapel (as stated above) of twisted wattles in the thirty-first year after the Lord's passion." So John<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0054_fn_001">s</a></sup> of Tynemouth in his "Sanctilogium of Britain", and his copyist John Capgrave in the "New Legend of England" — though with this prologue prudently set first: "What is digested below I excerpted at Glastonbury from the books of that monastery; whether these things savour of the truth, I leave to the reader's judgement." And with this epilogue added at the end: "For my part, in the doubtful matters written above, and in some of those that follow, I do not presume to myself the authority of examining and determining; but as a simple reporter I communicate without grudging what I, a diligent investigator, was able with labour and difficulty to find in diverse books and places, leaving the harder questions to be sifted by the learned."
