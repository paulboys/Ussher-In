# Page p0062

## Interlinear

*⟵ The opening lines of this page complete the final sentence of p0061 (seg_p0061_s0003), translated there. This page's own text begins below at l0017.*

**LA**  Probabilitatem contrariam, quam secundo loco urget, negamus omnino esse probabilem. Neque enim aliquem hodie, si Thomam Dempsterum exceperis<sup id="fnref-p0062-u"><a href="#fn-seg_p0062_fn_001">u</a></sup>, tam stulte credulum esse existimamus, cui absurdissimum illud commentum, de Josepho muro incluso, probabile videri possit: et de legendæ aureæ authore Jacobo de Voragine, ex<sup id="fnref-p0062-w"><a href="#fn-seg_p0062_fn_002">w</a></sup> cujus areolis fœtidi isti sanctorum flores sunt decerpti, in ipsa Hispania a Ludovico Vive<sup id="fnref-p0062-x"><a href="#fn-seg_p0062_fn_003">x</a></sup> et Melchiore Cano<sup id="fnref-p0062-y"><a href="#fn-seg_p0062_fn_004">y</a></sup> jamdudum lata est sententia; ferrei oris eum virum fuisse, atque plumbei cordis.

**EN**  The contrary probability which he urges in the second place, I deny to be probable at all; for I do not think that anyone today — unless you except Thomas Dempster<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0062_fn_001">u</a></sup> — is so foolishly credulous that that most absurd fiction of Joseph shut up within a wall could seem probable to him; and as for Jacobus de Voragine, the author of the "Golden Legend", from<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0062_fn_002">w</a></sup> whose garden-beds those fetid flowers of the saints were plucked, sentence was passed long ago in Spain itself by Juan Luis Vives<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0062_fn_003">x</a></sup> and Melchior Cano<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0062_fn_004">y</a></sup>: that he was a man of an iron mouth and a leaden heart.

**LA**  Tertium illud, quod totum Britanniæ regnum Josephi ministerio fidem non susceperit; verum quidem est, sed ad rem non pertinet. Nam nec totum regnum Hispaniæ a Jacobo conversum fuisse ipsi Hispani dicent; nec integra regna ab ullo apostolorum Christiana facta esse constat: et de antiquitate, non de universalitate susceptionis fidei instituta est quæstio.

**EN**  The third point — that the whole kingdom of Britain did not receive the faith through Joseph's ministry — is indeed true, but it does not bear on the matter; for the Spaniards themselves will not say that the whole kingdom of Spain was converted by James, nor is it established that entire kingdoms were made Christian by any of the apostles: and the question at issue concerns the antiquity, not the universality, of the reception of the faith.

**LA**  Quartum vero et postremum, partim incertis, partim falsis nititur fundamentis. Jacobi prædicationem in Hispania, inter incerta posuit Rodericus Ximenius Toletanus archiepiscopus, in concertatione quam anno MCCXV. in Lateranensi concilio cum præsule Compostellano de primatu habuit. Ejus verba, a Garsia Loaisa e libro MS. bibliothecæ Toletanæ edita, hæc sunt. “ Si<sup id="fnref-p0062-z"><a href="#fn-seg_p0063_fn_001">z</a></sup> inducat (Compostellanus) primam divini verbi promulgationem, et plurimorum conversionem ad fidem Christi in Hispania (per Jacobum factam:) qui divinam paginam noverunt, testimonium perhibeant. Ego tantum legi, datam ei fuisse potestatem prædicandi in Hispania: sed interim, cum per Judæam et Samariam divinam legem seminaret, sub Herode Hierosolymis truncato capite exhalavit animam, et Domino reddidit. Quomodo ergo ibi prædicavit, quo nondum ingressus est? aut sine prædicatione, quos, obsecro, convertit ad Dominum?” Nec ex parte altera certum est, quantumvis id ita esse affirmet Alphonsus, “ quod si Joseph fuisset in Anglia, hoc fuisset postquam de carcere liberatus fuisset per Titum, et sic post octuagesimum annum a nativitate Domini.” A Tito enim, anno æræ Christianæ (non octogesimo sed) septuagesimo eversam esse Hierosolymam certum est: quæ vero de Josepho Arimathæensi tunc per eum liberato pro certis assumuntur, nugæ sunt nugerrimæ.

**EN**  The fourth and last point rests on foundations partly uncertain and partly false: Rodrigo Jiménez, archbishop of Toledo, placed James's preaching in Spain among things uncertain, in the dispute over primacy which he held with the prelate of Compostela at the Lateran Council in the year 1215, and his words, published by García de Loaisa from a manuscript book in the library of Toledo, are these: "If<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0063_fn_001">z</a></sup> he (the prelate of Compostela) should adduce the first promulgation of the divine word and the conversion of very many people to the faith of Christ in Spain (as accomplished by James), let those who know the divine page bear witness; I have only read that power was given to him to preach in Spain, but meanwhile, while he was sowing the divine law through Judaea and Samaria, he was beheaded at Jerusalem under Herod, breathed out his soul, and rendered it to the Lord — how then did he preach there, where he had not yet set foot? or without preaching, whom, I ask, did he convert to the Lord?" — nor on the other side is it certain, however much Alphonsus affirms it to be so, "that if Joseph was in England, this would have been after he had been freed from prison by Titus, and thus after the eightieth year from the Lord's nativity"; for it is certain that Jerusalem was overthrown by Titus in the seventieth (not the eightieth) year of the Christian era, and the things assumed as certain about Joseph of Arimathea being freed by him at that time are the most trifling of trifles.
