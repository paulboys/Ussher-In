**EN**  But I would abuse both my own leisure and the reader’s patience if I were to recount the utterly trifling fictions about Joseph that are reported out of straw writings of this kind, both by Harding and Capgrave and in the great tablet of the monks of Glastonbury. One thing, however, must not be passed over: in the general synods of our Europeans, whenever controversy was conducted with the French and the Spaniards over the dignity of the British kingdom, the orators of the English nation repeatedly made a practice of appealing to this tradition about Joseph. This question was debated in the year 1409, first at the Council of Pisa, and then in the year 1417 at the Council of Constance; from which was excerpted the “Most Noble Disputation on the Dignity and Greatness of the Kingdoms of Britain and France, Held by the Orators and Legates of Both at the Council of Constance,” printed at Louvain in the year 1517. Sir Robert Wingfield, knight, ambassador of Henry VIII, king of England, to the Emperor Maximilian, had it published from the acts of the council preserved in the city of Constance itself; I have also found it in two manuscript<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0059_fn_001">r</a></sup> copies of the same council.
