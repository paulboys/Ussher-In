# Page p0058

## Interlinear

*⟵ The opening lines of this page complete the final sentence of p0057 (seg_p0057_s0002), translated there. This page's own text begins below at l0005.*

**LA**  Johannes Capgravius<sup id="fnref-p0058-i"><a href="#fn-seg_p0058_fn_001">i</a></sup>, in vita Josephi, his alium etiam testem addit Melkinum, quem ante Merlinum fuisse ait.

**EN**  John Capgrave<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0058_fn_001">i</a></sup>, in his "Life of Joseph", adds to these yet another witness, Melkin, who he says lived before Merlin.

**LA**  Ejus hæc producuntur verba, quæ ex variis<sup id="fnref-p0058-k"><a href="#fn-seg_p0058_fn_002">k</a></sup> exemplaribus descripta hic exhibemus, licet quæ legantur prorsus indigna. “Insula Avallonis avida funere paganorum præ cæteris in orbe ad sepulturam eorum omnium sperulis prophetiæ vaticinantibus decorata, et in futurum ornata, erit altissimum laudantibus. Abbadare<sup id="fnref-p0058-l"><a href="#fn-seg_p0058_fn_003">l</a></sup> potens in Saphat<sup id="fnref-p0058-m"><a href="#fn-seg_p0058_fn_004">m</a></sup>, paganorum nobilissimus, cum centum et quatuor millibus, dormitionem ibi accepit. Inter quos Joseph de Marmore<sup id="fnref-p0058-n"><a href="#fn-seg_p0058_fn_005">n</a></sup> (ab Arimathia nomine) cepit somnum perpetuum. Et jacet in linea bifurcata juxta meridianum angulum oratorii cratibus præparatæ<sup id="fnref-p0058-o"><a href="#fn-seg_p0058_fn_006">o</a></sup> super potentem adorandam virginem, supradictis sperulatis locum habentibus<sup id="fnref-p0058-p"><a href="#fn-seg_p0058_fn_007">p</a></sup> tredecim. Habet enim secum Joseph in sarcophago duo vascula alba et argentea, cruore prophetiæ Jesu et sudore perimpleta.

**EN**  These words of his are brought forward, which I present here as copied from various<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0058_fn_002">k</a></sup> copies, although what is read there is utterly unworthy: "The Isle of Avalon, eager for the burial of pagans, adorned beyond all others in the world, for the burial of them all, with prophesying spherules of prophecy, and decorated for the future, will be most exalted for those who praise it. Abbadare<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0058_fn_003">l</a></sup>, mighty in Saphat<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0058_fn_004">m</a></sup>, noblest of the pagans, received his sleep there with one hundred and four thousand. Among them Joseph de Marmore<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0058_fn_005">n</a></sup> (named from Arimathea) took his perpetual sleep. And he lies on a forked line next to the southern corner of the oratory prepared with wattles<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0058_fn_006">o</a></sup>, over the mighty and adorable Virgin, the aforesaid thirteen sphere-marked ones occupying the place<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0058_fn_007">p</a></sup>. For Joseph has with him in the sarcophagus two white and silver vessels, filled to the brim with the blood of the prophecy of Jesus and with his sweat.

**LA**  Cum reperietur ejus sarcophagum integrum, illibatum, in futuris temporibus videbitur; et erit apertum toto orbi terrarum. Et tunc nec aqua nec ros cœli insulam nobilissimam habitantibus poterit deficere. Per multum tempus ante diem judicialem in Josaphat erant aperta hæc et viventibus declarata.” Hunc Mevinum Britannum chronographum appellat Johannes Hardingus in chronico suo poemate<sup id="fnref-p0058-q"><a href="#fn-seg_p0059_fn_003">q</a></sup>, pro quo Nenii nomen, capite octavo et quadragesimo irrepsit : ubi bellam istam ex eo recitat fabellam, quod Josephus Arvirago regi pro gentis Britannicæ insignibus crucem, quæ Georgiana postea dicta est, in clypeo argenteo depictam tradiderit.

**EN**  When his sarcophagus is found whole and undisturbed, it will be seen in future times, and it will be open to the whole world. And then neither water nor the dew of heaven will be able to fail those who inhabit the most noble isle. For a long time before the day of judgement in Josaphat these things were open and declared to the living." John Harding, in his chronicle in verse<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0059_fn_003">q</a></sup>, calls this man Mevinus, a British chronographer — in place of whose name that of Nennius has crept in, at chapter 48 — where he recounts from him that pretty fable, that Joseph delivered to King Arviragus, as the arms of the British nation, the cross afterwards called the cross of St George, depicted on a silver shield.

**EN:**  And another, older than he, in the margin of a manuscript of Matthew the Florilegist in the library of Sir James Ware: "Melkinus (or rather Mewynus), who lived before Merlin, among the other deeds of the times of the Britons, says this in his book: 'Joseph of Arimathea, a noble decurion, took his perpetual sleep in the isle of Avalon with his eleven companions; and he lies in the southern corner of the forked line of the oratory of the adorable Virgin. For he has with him two white silver vessels, filled to the brim with the blood and sweat of the great prophet Jesus. And for a long time before the Day of Judgement his body will be found whole and undisturbed, and it will be open to the whole world. Then neither dew nor rain will fail those who inhabit the most noble isle.'"
