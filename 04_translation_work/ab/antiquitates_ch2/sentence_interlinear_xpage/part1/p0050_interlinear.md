## Interlinear

*⟵ The opening lines of this page complete the final sentence of p0049 (seg_p0049_s0001), translated there. This page's own text begins below at l0007.*

**LA**  Quod vero Josephi Arimathæensis opera in gentibus Britannicis ad fidem perducendis Philippus usus fuerit, id totum ex Glastoniensium monachorum lacunis haustum est: a quibus et barbari illi manarunt versiculi, ex chronicis quibusdam de rege Arvirago agentibus citati<sup id="fnref-p0050-g"><a href="#fn-seg_p0050_fn_001">g</a></sup>: Intrat Avalloniam duodena caterva virorum; Flos Arimathææ Joseph est primus eorum.

**EN**  But that Philip made use of the services of Joseph of Arimathea in bringing the British peoples to the faith is drawn entirely from the muddy pools of the Glastonbury monks, from whom also flowed those barbarous little verses, cited from certain chronicles treating of King Arviragus<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0050_fn_001">g</a></sup>: 'A twelvefold band of men enters Avalonia; Joseph, the flower of Arimathea, is the first of them.'

**LA**  Josephes ex Joseph genitus patrem comitatur; His aliisque decem jus Glastoniæ propriatur.

**EN**  'Josephes, begotten of Joseph, accompanies his father; to these and ten others the right of Glastonbury is appropriated.'

**LA**  Atque ex<sup id="fnref-p0050-h"><a href="#fn-seg_p0050_fn_002">h</a></sup> aliis, paris elegantiæ, isti: Joseph cum sociis jussit transire Philippus Ad terram Britonum divinum promere verbum.

**EN**  And from<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0050_fn_002">h</a></sup> others, of equal elegance, these: 'Philip ordered Joseph with his companions to cross over to the land of the Britons, to bring forth the divine word.'

**LA**  Fert<sup id="fnref-p0050-i"><a href="#fn-seg_p0050_fn_003">i</a></sup> hos camisia qui promissum tenuerunt: Navigio ceteri terræ mox applicuerunt.

**EN**  'A shirt carries<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0050_fn_003">i</a></sup> those who kept their promise: the rest soon made land by ship.'

**LA**  Regi consuluit Joseph tunc credere Christum: Arviragus renuit rex hoc, nec credit in ipsum.

**EN**  'Joseph then counseled the king to believe in Christ: King Arviragus refused this, and did not believe in him.'

**LA**  Glastoniæ bis sex hidas dedit Arviragus rex: Joseph cum sociis jura reliquit eis.

**EN**  'King Arviragus gave twice six hides of Glastonbury to Joseph and his companions, and left the rights to them.'

**LA**  Anno trigeno primo post vulnera Christi, Ecclesiam virgis faciunt cum laudibus isti.

**EN**  'In the thirty-first year after the wounds of Christ, these men, with praises, build a church of wattles.'

**LA**  Habetur et hodie Welliæ in ædibus D. Thomæ Hugonis equitis aurati, tabula ænea, columnæ Glastoniensis ecclesiæ olim affixa: cui incisum legitur: “ Anno post passionem Domini XXXI. duodecim sancti, ex quibus Joseph ab Arimathia primus erat, huc venerunt, qui Ecclesiam hujus regni primam in hoc loco construxerunt, quam Christus in honorem suæ matris et locum pro eorum sepultura præsentialiter dedicavit.” Est etiam penes nobilissimum virum, D. Guilielmum Howardum, Thomæ Norfolciæ Ducis filium, ingens tabula, Glastonienses antiquitates undique conquisitas complectens : in qua, inter alia fabulosissima, et ista legimus : “ Joseph ab Arimathia beati Philippi apostoli discipulatui se contulit, atque ejus salubri doctrina aﬄuenter refertus et ab ipso cum filio suo Josepho baptizatus est. Postea vero a beato Johanne apostolo ipsi prædicationi Ephesiorum insudante, beatæ perpetuæque virginis Mariæ paranymphus delegatus est : ejusdem quoque gloriosæ virginis venerandæ assumptioni cum beato Philippo cæterisque discipulis interfuit, atque ea quæ de Domino Jesu Christo atque ejus gloriosa genitrice Maria audierat et viderat instanter per diversas regiones prædicans, ac multos convertens et baptizans ; tandem decimo quinto post eandem gloriosam assumptionem anno, cum memorato filio suo Josepho ad sanctum Philippum in Gallias venit. Ac deinde secundum quod legitur in libro qui dicitur Graal, Joseph ab Arimathia, qui dominum sepelivit, baptizatus a beato Philippo apostolo venit in majorem Britanniam, sibi et semini ejus promissam, cum uxore, et filio Josephe nomine, quem ipse Salvator noster in civitate Saraz prius in episcopum consecraverat.”

**EN**  There is preserved even today at Wells, in the house of Sir Thomas Hughes, knight, a bronze tablet, once affixed to a column of the church of Glastonbury, on which the following inscription is read: 'In the thirty-first year after the passion of the Lord, twelve saints, of whom Joseph of Arimathea was the first, came to this place, and they built the first Church of this kingdom on this spot, which Christ in his own presence dedicated in honor of his mother and as a place for their burial.' There is also in the possession of that most noble man, Lord William Howard, son of Thomas, Duke of Norfolk, an immense tablet comprising Glastonbury antiquities gathered from every quarter, in which, among other utterly fabulous matter, I read this as well: 'Joseph of Arimathea devoted himself to the discipleship of the blessed apostle Philip, and, abundantly filled with his wholesome doctrine, was baptized by him together with his son Josephes. Afterwards, while the blessed apostle John was laboring at the preaching to the Ephesians, he was appointed paranymph of the blessed and perpetual virgin Mary; he was also present, with the blessed Philip and the other disciples, at the venerable assumption of the same glorious virgin, and, earnestly preaching through diverse regions the things he had heard and seen concerning the Lord Jesus Christ and his glorious mother Mary, and converting and baptizing many, at length, in the fifteenth year after that same glorious assumption, he came with his aforementioned son Josephes to Saint Philip in Gaul. And then, according to what is read in the book called the Graal, Joseph of Arimathea, who buried the Lord, having been baptized by the blessed apostle Philip, came into Greater Britain, promised to him and his seed, with his wife and a son named Josephes, whom our Savior himself had earlier consecrated bishop in the city of Saraz.'
