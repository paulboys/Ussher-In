**LA**  CAP. II. De duodecim Philippi discipulis, et Josepho Arimathæensi, Glastoniensium monachorum traditiones; veterum nominibus fucatæ, et in Europæorum generalibus conciliis venditatæ.

**EN**  CHAPTER II. The traditions of the Glastonbury monks concerning the twelve disciples of Philip and Joseph of Arimathea; dressed up with the names of the ancients, and paraded at the general councils of Europe.

**LA**  AD Glastoniensium monachorum, de Josepho Arimathæensi et ejus sociis, traditiones nunc venio: quas etsi Normannorum adventu antiquiores non existimem, et posteriorum temporum superstitionem plane redoleant; tamen qualescunque sunt, ex Guilielmi Malmesburiensis de antiquitate Glastoniensis ecclesiæ libello exhibere visum est.

**EN**  I now come to the traditions of the Glastonbury monks concerning Joseph of Arimathea and his companions: although I do not consider them older than the arrival of the Normans, and they plainly smack of the superstition of later times, nevertheless, such as they are, I have thought it fitting to present them from William of Malmesbury's little treatise "On the Antiquity of the Church of Glastonbury".

**LA**  Sic igitur ille, præmissa præfatione ad Henricum Blesensem, Henrici I. regis ex Adela sorore nepotem, Wintoniensem tum episcopum et Glastoniensem abbatem, archæologiam suam ab apostolicis exorditur temporibus.

**EN**  Thus, then, having first set out a preface addressed to Henry of Blois — nephew of King Henry I by his sister Adela, and at that time bishop of Winchester and abbot of Glastonbury — he (William of Malmesbury) begins his account of antiquities from apostolic times.

**EN**  "After the glory of the Lord's resurrection, the triumph of his ascension, and the sending from on high of the Spirit, the Paraclete (who filled the disciples' hearts, still trembling with fear of temporal punishment, by bestowing on them the knowledge of all tongues), all the believers were together with the women and Mary the mother of Jesus, as Luke the evangelist relates; and the word of God was spread abroad, and the number of believers grew daily, and they all had one heart and one soul. The priests of the Jews, therefore, together with the Pharisees and Scribes, inflamed by the torches of envy, stirred up a persecution against the Church, killing the protomartyr Stephen and driving nearly all the believers far from their borders. While this storm of persecution raged, the scattered believers made for the various kingdoms of the earth allotted to them by the Lord, offering the word of salvation to the peoples.
