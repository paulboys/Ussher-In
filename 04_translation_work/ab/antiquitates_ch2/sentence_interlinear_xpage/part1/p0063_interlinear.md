## Interlinear

*⟵ The opening lines of this page complete the final sentence of p0062 (seg_p0062_s0003), translated there. This page's own text begins below at l0025.*

**LA**  Guilielmus<sup id="fnref-p0063-a"><a href="#fn-seg_p0063_fn_002">a</a></sup> quidem Eysengreineus ex annalibus Anglorum narrat eum “ post urbis Hierosolymæ destructionem apostolis adhæsisse; atque hoc pacto jam senem in Britanniam profectum, in urbe Wellia Christum prædicavisse.” Sed temporis illam circumstantiam in nullis Anglorum annalibus expressam reperit. Glastonienses, et Malmesburiensis monachus in secundo Eulogii sui libro, Josephi adventum ad annum Christi LXIII. referunt: quem ab assumptione beatæ virginis XV. fuisse tradunt.

**EN**  William<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0063_fn_002">a</a></sup> Eysengrein, it is true, relates from the annals of the English that he (Joseph) "adhered to the apostles after the destruction of the city of Jerusalem, and that in this way, now an old man, he set out for Britain and preached Christ in the city of Wells"; but one finds that circumstance of time expressed in none of the annals of the English. The monks of Glastonbury, and the monk of Malmesbury in the second book of his "Eulogium", assign Joseph's arrival to the year of Christ 63, which they report to have been the fifteenth year from the assumption of the Blessed Virgin.

**LA**  Oratores Angli, ut diximus, in Basileensi hoc concilio, templum a Josepho in Britannia erectum asserunt, “ tempore quo Petrus in Antiochia prædicavit fidem :” et in Constantiensi, “ statim post passionem Christi” eundem Britanniam ingressum esse, et populos ad fidem convertisse defendunt. Quod et cum ipsius Josephi ætate, qui Domino passionem adeunte inter Judæos senator<sup id="fnref-p0063-b"><a href="#fn-seg_p0064_fn_001">b</a></sup> honoratus fuerat, et cum iis quæ initio ex Gilda de tempore summo Tiberii Cæsaris produximus, bene quadrat.

**EN**  The English orators, as I have said, assert in this Council of Basel that a church was erected by Joseph in Britain "at the time when Peter preached the faith in Antioch"; and at the Council of Constance they maintain that the same man entered Britain "immediately after the passion of Christ" and converted its peoples to the faith — which agrees well both with the age of Joseph himself, who, when the Lord went to his passion, had been an honored senator<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0064_fn_001">b</a></sup> among the Jews, and with what I brought forward at the beginning from Gildas concerning the latter end of the reign of Tiberius Caesar.

**EN:**  Mark 15:43, εὐσχήμων βουλευτής [an honourable councillor]; which the Vulgate translator renders nobilis decurio [a noble decurion], on which see the commentary of St. Jerome on Matt. 27, that of the distinguished Ludovicus de Dieu on Mark 15, and that of Hugo Grotius on Matt. 27:57. Those who in Pliny the Younger, book 10, epistle 113, are called Buleutae are in Trajan's rescript, epistle 114, called decuriones.
