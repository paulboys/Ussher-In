## Interlinear

*⟵ The opening lines of this page complete the final sentence of p0064 (seg_p0064_s0001), translated there. This page's own text begins below at l0003.*

**LA**  Extat vetustus catalogus sanctorum in Anglia sepultorum Saxonice scriptus, ac Latine, a Gotcelino Bertiniano monacho Cantuariensi, Anselmi archiepiscopi diebus editus: et de reliquis in Glastoniensi monasterio repositis libellus alter, Henrici III. Anglorum regis temporibus exaratus. In neutro vero Josephi ulla occurrit mentio.

**EN**  There survives an ancient catalogue of the saints buried in England, written in Saxon and in Latin, published by Goscelin of Saint-Bertin, a monk of Canterbury, in the days of Archbishop Anselm; and a second little book, concerning the relics deposited in the monastery of Glastonbury, drawn up in the times of Henry III, king of the English; yet in neither does any mention of Joseph occur.

**LA**  Anno demum MCCCXLV. a Johanne Blomæo Londinensi, cujusdam revelationis sibi factæ prætextu, ab Edvardo III. licentia quærendi corpus Josephi ab Arimathia impetrata est: quam, ex archivis regiis<sup id="fnref-p0065-f"><a href="#fn-seg_p0065_fn_002">f</a></sup> in arce Londinensi asservatis a me descriptam, lectori communicare non gravabor.

**EN**  At length, in the year 1345, John Blome of London, on the pretext of a certain revelation made to him, obtained from Edward III a licence to search for the body of Joseph of Arimathea; and this licence, which I have copied from the royal archives<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0065_fn_002">f</a></sup> preserved in the Tower of London, I gladly communicate to the reader.

**LA**  “Rex omnibus ad quos, &c. salutem. Supplicavit nobis Johannes Blome de London, ut cum sibi (sicut asserit) divinitus sit injunctum, ut venerandum corpus decurionis nobilis Josephi ab Arimathia, quod infra septa monasterii Glastoniensis in Christo quiescit humatum, et est ad honorem et multorum ædificationem his temporibus revelandum, quærat donec inveniat diligenter. Et quia in quibusdam antiquis scripturis dicitur contineri corpus ejus ibidem fuisse sepultum. Nos, si sit ita, desiderantes monumentum ejus, et venerandas ipsius reliquias, qui redemptori nostro morienti tantum exhibebat pietatis et humanitatis obsequium, corpus ejus de cruce deponendo, et illud in monumento suo novo ponendo, devotis honoribus prævenire; et sperantes nobis et toti regno nostro ex revelatione prædicta gratiam uberiorem provenire: concessimus et licentiam dedimus (quantum in nobis est) eidem Johanni quod ipse infra præcinctum dicti monasterii fodere, et illas prætiosas reliquias juxta injunctionem et revelationem sibi factam quærere valeat in locis ubi melius viderit expedire: dum tamen absque damno dilectorum nobis in Christo, abbatis et conventus dicti monasterii, et ruina ecclesiæ et domorum suarum ibidem id fieri valeat; et quod ad id ipsorum abbatis et conventus licentiam habeat et assensum. In cujus, &c. Teste Rege PER IPSUM REGEM.

**EN**  "The King to all to whom, etc., greeting. John Blome of London has petitioned us that — since (as he asserts) it has been divinely enjoined upon him that he diligently search, until he find it, for the venerable body of the noble decurion Joseph of Arimathea, which rests buried in Christ within the precincts of the monastery of Glastonbury and is in these times to be revealed for the honour and edification of many, and because it is said to be recorded in certain ancient writings that his body was buried in that place — we, if it be so, desiring to honour beforehand with devout observances his monument and the venerable relics of him who showed to our dying Redeemer so great a service of piety and humanity in taking his body down from the cross and laying it in his own new tomb, and hoping that from the said revelation more abundant grace may flow to us and to our whole realm, have granted and given licence (so far as in us lies) to the same John, that he may dig within the precinct of the said monastery and search for those precious relics, according to the injunction and revelation made to him, in the places where he shall see it most expedient; provided, however, that this can be done without harm to our beloved in Christ, the abbot and convent of the said monastery, and without ruin to the church and their buildings there; and that he have for this the licence and assent of the abbot and convent themselves. In witness whereof, etc. Witness the King. BY THE KING HIMSELF."
