**LA**  Dicitur autem hida tanta terræ portio, quanta ad familiam alendam sufficiat (unde quod Beda in ecclesiastica historia familiam vocat, Saxonicus illius interpres Hẏdelandeꞃ vertit) vel quanta unico per annum coli possit aratro; quemadmodum tum ex Dunstani charta anno DCCCCLXIII. data, in qua terræ partem concedit “ septem aratrorum<sup id="fnref-p0056-z"><a href="#fn-seg_p0056_fn_002">z</a></sup>, quod Anglice dicitur septem hidas :” tum ex Matthæo Parisiensi, Henrico Huntindoniensi, Johanne Londinensi, Thoma Walsinghamo, et aliis nostri observant antiquarii<sup id="fnref-p0056-a"><a href="#fn-seg_p0056_fn_003">a</a></sup>. Tres vero istos reges paganos, Arviragum, Marium et Coillum fuisse, ad marginem libri Malmesburiensis, qui MS. habetur in bibliotheca collegii S. Trinitatis Cantabrigiæ, annotatum invenio: quod ipsum etiam innuit Johannes Capgravius; quanquam parum hic sibi constans. In vita enim Patricii, Johannem Tinmuthensem secutus et Hardingum in chronico<sup id="fnref-p0056-b"><a href="#fn-seg_p0057_fn_001">b</a></sup>, duodecim istas hidas ab Arvirago concessas fuisse scribit; in Josephi vero vita, a duobus illius successoribus, Mario Arviragi, et Coillo Marii filio. Quos tamen reges Johannes Hardingus paganos extitisse vix admittit. Arviragum enim a Josepho Arimathæensi<sup id="fnref-p0056-c"><a href="#fn-seg_p0057_fn_002">c</a></sup> ad fidem conversum et salutari baptismo ablutum fuisse narrat: Marium<sup id="fnref-p0056-d"><a href="#fn-seg_p0057_fn_003">d</a></sup> vero et Coillum religione Christiana aliquantum imbutos, licet ad eam quæ par erat perfectionem non pertigerint. Quomodo et Richardus<sup id="fnref-p0056-e"><a href="#fn-seg_p0057_fn_004">e</a></sup> Vitus Basinstochius, Arviragum “ erga religionem Christianam bene aﬀectum” fuisse ait: Cœlum quoque, illius ex filio Mario nepotem, “instructum religione Christiana parumper dum viveret, non tamen ut id publice præ se ferret.”

**EN**  Now a hide is said to be as great a portion of land as suffices to support one family (whence what Bede in the "Ecclesiastical History" calls a familia, his Saxon translator renders Hẏdelandeꞃ), or as much as can be cultivated in a year by a single plough; as our antiquaries<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0056_fn_003">a</a></sup> observe, both from a charter of Dunstan issued in the year 963, in which he grants a portion of land "of seven ploughs<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0056_fn_002">z</a></sup>, which in English is called seven hides," and from Matthew Paris, Henry of Huntingdon, John of London, Thomas Walsingham, and others. But that those three kings — Arviragus, Marius, and Coillus — were pagans, I find noted in the margin of the book of Malmesbury which is held in manuscript in the library of Trinity College, Cambridge; and John Capgrave also intimates the same, although he is hardly consistent with himself on this point. For in the life of Patrick, following John of Tynemouth and Harding in his chronicle<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0057_fn_001">b</a></sup>, he writes that those twelve hides were granted by Arviragus; but in the life of Joseph, that they were granted by two of his successors, Marius the son of Arviragus, and Coillus the son of Marius. Yet John Harding scarcely admits that these kings were pagans. For he relates that Arviragus was converted to the faith by Joseph of Arimathea<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0057_fn_002">c</a></sup> and washed in saving baptism, and that Marius<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0057_fn_003">d</a></sup> and Coillus were to some degree imbued with the Christian religion, although they did not attain to the perfection that was fitting. In the same way Richard<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0057_fn_004">e</a></sup> White of Basingstoke also says that Arviragus was "well disposed toward the Christian religion," and that Coelus too, his grandson by his son Marius, was "instructed for a time in the Christian religion while he lived, yet not so as to profess it publicly."
