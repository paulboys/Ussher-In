**LA**  Multa vero inde in fabulosa regis Arthuri acta, lingua Anglicana a se edita, transtulit Thomas Malorius: qui Sangreal vocem hic usurpat, ad sanguinis realis notionem proxime accedentem; partem<sup id="fnref-p0052-l"><a href="#fn-seg_p0052_fn_001">l</a></sup> enim pretiosissimi sanguinis Servatoris nostri repoſitum hic fuisse fingit: sive in cruce eﬀusi, sive ex suprema Chriſti cœna reservati: de qua Stephanus Forcatulus: “ Britanni<sup id="fnref-p0052-m"><a href="#fn-seg_p0052_fn_002">m</a></sup> sacræ illius cœnæ pignus ac testimonium secum attulisse Josephum commemorant: nempe catinum sanctissimis convivis appositum, diutissime in magna ceremonia, tanquam pignus quoddam securitatis Britannicæ; non aliter atque Trojanum Palladium.” Hæc ille: de aucta etiam a Josepho vetere magia Britannica eﬀatum non minus a ratione quam a religione remotum postea adjiciens: “ Auxit<sup id="fnref-p0052-n"><a href="#fn-seg_p0052_fn_003">n</a></sup> prodigiosos ritus Josephus Arimathæus, advena Britanniæ plauſsbilis; qui de Solomonis fonte artem hauserat. Fuit namque hic sapiens rex imbutus divinitus secretiore sapientia: incantationes<sup id="fnref-p0052-o"><a href="#fn-seg_p0052_fn_004">o</a></sup> composuit, quibus morbi pellerentur, et dæmones fugarentur, non redituri.”

**EN**  But Thomas Malory carried much over from that source into the fabulous deeds of King Arthur which he published in the English tongue: he there employs the word Sangreal, which comes very near the notion of 'real blood' (sanguis realis); for he feigns that a portion<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0052_fn_001">l</a></sup> of the most precious blood of our Saviour was deposited here — whether shed on the cross, or reserved from Christ's Last Supper — concerning which Stephanus Forcatulus writes: "The Britons<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0052_fn_002">m</a></sup> record that Joseph brought with him a pledge and testimony of that sacred supper: namely, the dish that was set before those most holy guests, kept for a very long time with great ceremony, as a kind of pledge of Britain's security, just like the Trojan Palladium." So much from him; he afterwards adds, concerning the ancient British magic as likewise augmented by Joseph, a pronouncement no less removed from reason than from religion: "Joseph of Arimathea, a plausible newcomer to Britain, augmented<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0052_fn_003">n</a></sup> their prodigious rites; he had drawn his art from the fountain of Solomon. For that wise king was divinely imbued with a more secret wisdom: he composed incantations<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0052_fn_004">o</a></sup> by which diseases were driven out and demons put to flight, never to return."

**LA**  De duodecim discipulis a B. Philippo in Britanniam missis, Malmesburiensis quidam monachus in eulogii sui libro secundo hunc in modum meminit: “ Anno Incarnationis Domini LXIII. et assumptionis beatæ virginis Mariæ XV. duodecim discipuli venerunt in Britanniam majorem, missi a beato Philippo apostolo ad verbum Dei ibidem prædicandum: quorum primus fuit Joseph ab Arimathia, qui Jesum Christum sepelivit. Quibus rex Arviragus, licet paganus, ne ulterius laborarent, duodecim hidas terræ eis contulit in insula Avalloniæ, quæ nunc dicitur Glastonia. In quo loco ecclesiam in honore beatæ virginis per admonitionem S. Michaelis archangeli, de virgis torquatis muros facientes, construxerunt."

**EN**  Concerning the twelve disciples sent into Britain by the blessed Philip, a certain monk of Malmesbury makes mention in the second book of his "Eulogium" in this manner: "In the year of the Lord's Incarnation 63, and the fifteenth from the assumption of the blessed Virgin Mary, twelve disciples came into Greater Britain, sent by the blessed apostle Philip to preach the word of God there; the first of whom was Joseph of Arimathea, who buried Jesus Christ. To them King Arviragus, though a pagan, granted twelve hides of land in the isle of Avalon, which is now called Glastonbury, so that they should labor no further. In that place, at the admonition of St. Michael the archangel, they built a church in honor of the blessed Virgin, making its walls of wattled twigs."
