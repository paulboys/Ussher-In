## Interlinear

*⟵ The opening lines of this page complete the final sentence of p0063 (seg_p0063_s0002), translated there. This page's own text begins below at l0008.*

**LA**  Johannes enim Hardingius indignus est, cujus hic ulla habeatur ratio : qui<sup id="fnref-p0064-c"><a href="#fn-seg_p0064_fn_002">c</a></sup> Josephum per ipsos quadraginta duos a Christi morte annos, non quidem in muro, sed in Caiphæ carcere detentum, sine omni cibo et potu vixisse ; deinde a Vespasiano liberatum, et in Britanniam anno Christi LXXVI. perductum, ibique regi Arvirago et conjugi ipsius Genissæ (quæ Claudii imperatoris filia fuisse fingitur) commendatum fuisse fabulatur. Johannes Balæus, Ossoriensis aliquando apud Hibernos episcopus, eodem illo anno ex vita hac migrasse eum scribit. “ In sancta senectute (inquit<sup id="fnref-p0064-d"><a href="#fn-seg_p0064_fn_003">d</a></sup>) Josephus inter discipulos tandem quietam morte carnis dormitionem accepit, in Avalonica solitudine sepultus, anno a Christi incarnatione LXXVI. Mario regnante in Britannia. Postea translatum est ejus corpus ad Glasconiense monachorum collegium, et in pretiosa theca repositum. Neque desunt (inquit ille) qui ex conjecturis habeant, contexuisse illum ad ecclesias Britannorum epistolas quasdam.” Quam conjecturam ut verisimiliorem redderet Guilielmus Eysengreineus, ne a præsente ad præsentes literæ scriptæ putarentur, “ reliquisse insulam et, ut renovaret sui memoriam, scripsisse epistolam ad Britannos” illum fingit: Anglorum hic quoque citatis annalibus ; in quibus tamen neque Britanniam eum reliquisse, neque ejusmodi aliquam epistolam scripsisse, neque alibi quam Glastoniæ mortuum et sepultum fuisse, uspiam traditum invenimus<sup id="fnref-p0064-e"><a href="#fn-seg_p0065_fn_001">e</a></sup>.

**EN**  For John Harding is unworthy of any consideration here — a man who<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0064_fn_002">c</a></sup> tells the fable that Joseph, detained for the full forty-two years after Christ's death not indeed in a wall but in the prison of Caiaphas, lived without any food or drink; that he was then freed by Vespasian and brought to Britain in the year of Christ 76, and there commended to King Arviragus and to his wife Genissa (who is feigned to have been a daughter of the emperor Claudius). John Bale, at one time bishop of Ossory among the Irish, writes that he departed this life in that same year. "In holy old age," he says,<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0064_fn_003">d</a></sup> "Joseph at last received among the disciples a quiet falling-asleep in the death of the flesh, and was buried in the solitude of Avalon, in the year 76 from the incarnation of Christ, while Marius was reigning in Britain. Afterwards his body was translated to the college of monks at Glastonbury and laid up in a precious reliquary. Nor are there lacking," he says, "those who hold by conjecture that he composed certain epistles to the churches of the Britons." To render this conjecture the more plausible, Wilhelm Eisengrein — lest the letter be thought written by one present to those present — feigns that he "left the island and, to renew the memory of himself, wrote an epistle to the Britons," here too citing the annals of the English; in which, however, I nowhere find it recorded that he left Britain, or that he wrote any epistle of this kind, or that he died and was buried anywhere but at Glastonbury<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0065_fn_001">e</a></sup>.
