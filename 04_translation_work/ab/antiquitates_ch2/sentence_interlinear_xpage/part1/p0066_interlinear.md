## Interlinear

*⟵ The opening lines of this page complete the final sentence of p0065 (seg_p0065_s0003), translated there. This page's own text begins below at l0006.*

**LA**  Apud Westmonaster.

**EN**  At Westminster.

**LA**  VIII. die Junii.

**EN**  On the eighth day of June.

**LA**  Quem habuerit eventum ista inquisitio, non invenio: ab his tamen qui Glastoniense monasterium viderunt est proditum, et sacellum ibi Josephi nomini dicatum, et tumulum etiam positum fuisse, hoc inscriptum epitaphio.

**EN**  What outcome that inquiry had, I do not find; yet it has been reported by those who have seen the monastery of Glastonbury that a chapel dedicated to Joseph's name stood there, and that a tomb also was placed there, inscribed with this epitaph.

**LA**  Ad Britones veni, postquam Christum sepelivi; -----------------------------------------docui, requievi.

**EN**  I came to the Britons after I had buried Christ; ----------------------------------------- I taught, I rested.

**LA**  Quibus adde, si libet, et Guilielmi Goodi Jesuitæ narrationem, qui Henrico VIII. regnante Glastoniæ natus et puer educatus est. " Glasconiæ<sup id="fnref-p0066-g"><a href="#fn-seg_p0066_fn_001">g</a></sup> extabant (inquit) laminæ æneæ sculptæ ad perpetuandam memoriam, sacella, crypta, cruces, arma, observatio festi S. Josephi ad VI. Calendas Augusti, quamdiu monachi regum chartis munitissime gaudebant: nunc omnia cum ruinis confusa perierunt.

**EN**  To these add, if you please, the account of William Good the Jesuit, who was born at Glastonbury in the reign of Henry VIII and brought up there as a boy: "At Glastonbury<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0066_fn_001">g</a></sup>," he says, "there existed engraved bronze plates to perpetuate the memory, chapels, a crypt, crosses, coats of arms, and the observance of the feast of St. Joseph on the sixth day before the Calends of August [27 July], so long as the monks enjoyed the fullest protection of royal charters; now all these, jumbled together with the ruins, have perished.

**LA**  Nemo tamen monachorum unquam scivit certum locum sepulchri hujus sancti, vel designavit. Reconditum abditissime dixerunt, vel ibi, vel in monte, qui monti acuto vicinus, et cui nomen Hamden-hill : et futurum, ut quando reperietur corpus ejus, universus eo mundus confluat præ magnitudine et numero miraculorum quæ fient."

**EN**  Yet none of the monks ever knew, or pointed out, the certain place of this saint's burial; they said it had been hidden away most secretly, either there or on a hill lying near the sharp hill (Montacute) and bearing the name Hamden-hill; and that when his body should be found, the whole world would flock there, on account of the greatness and number of the miracles that would be wrought."

**LA**  " Et inter cætera memini me vidisse Glasconiæ in cruce lapidea, quæ sub regina Elizabetha subversa fuit, laminam æneam in qua scribebatur, Josephum ab Arimathæa trigesimo anno post passionem Christi venisse in Britanniam cum undecim vel duodecim sociis, permissumque ei ab Arvirago rege, Glasconiæ quæ tunc erat insula dicta Avalonia, habitare, tanquam solitarios viros et simplices : et quod attulerit secum duo vasa argentea non grandia, in<sup id="fnref-p0066-h"><a href="#fn-seg_p0067_fn_001">h</a></sup> quibus erat de sanguine et aqua sacratissima, quæ profluxerat de latere Christi mortui. Crux autem ista erecta est ante multos annos ad denotandam longitudinem sacelli ab eodem sancto Josepho ex vimineis cratibus constructi in honorem beatissimæ virginis; deducta scilicet linea recta a medio illius crucis ad latus sacelli postea lapidibus quadratis exædificati: sub qua item erat olim illud sacellum Sancti Josephi in crypta subterranea. Foris autem in muro hujus sacelli beatæ virginis erant in lapide sculpta vetustissimis literis hæc duo verba, Jesus, Maria. Et confirmant antiqua arma Glasconiensis monasterii, quæ sunt hujusmodi: Scutum album in quo per longum erigitur stipes crucis viridis et nodosæ, et de latere ad latus extenduntur brachia seu rami crucis stipiti consimilia.

**EN**  "And among other things I remember that I saw at Glastonbury, on a stone cross which was thrown down under Queen Elizabeth, a bronze plate on which it was written that Joseph of Arimathea came into Britain in the thirtieth year after the passion of Christ with eleven or twelve companions; that he was permitted by King Arviragus to dwell at Glastonbury, which was then an island called Avalonia, as men solitary and simple; and that he brought with him two silver vessels, not large, in<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0067_fn_001">h</a></sup> which was some of the most sacred blood and water that had flowed from the side of the dead Christ. Now that cross was erected many years ago to mark the length of the chapel built by the same St. Joseph of wattled hurdles in honour of the most blessed Virgin — a straight line, that is, being drawn from the middle of that cross to the side of the chapel afterwards built up of squared stones; beneath which, likewise, that chapel of St. Joseph formerly lay in an underground crypt. On the outside, moreover, on the wall of this chapel of the blessed Virgin, these two words were carved in stone in most ancient letters: Jesus, Maria. And the ancient arms of the monastery of Glastonbury confirm this, which are of this kind: a white shield on which the trunk of a green and knotty cross is raised lengthwise, and from side to side extend the arms or branches of the cross, like the trunk.
