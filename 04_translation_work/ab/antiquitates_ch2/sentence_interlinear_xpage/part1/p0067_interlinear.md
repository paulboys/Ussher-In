**LA**  Sparguntur guttæ sanguinis per omnem aream scuti.

**EN**  Drops of blood are scattered over the whole field of the shield.

**LA**  Utrinque ad latera stipitis, et sub alis crucis, ponitur ampulla inaurata. Et hæc semper denominabantur insignia Sancti Josephi: qui ibi habitasse pie credebatur, et fortasse sepultus esse.”

**EN**  On either side of the upright, and beneath the arms of the cross, is placed a gilded cruet; and these were always called the arms of Saint Joseph, who was piously believed to have lived there, and perhaps to have been buried there."

**LA**  Eundem cum sociis ibi fuisse sepultum, in magna Glastoniensium tabula ita traditum invenio. “ Ibi requiescunt XII. discipuli: quorum primas et custos fuit Joseph ab Arimathia, qui Dominum sepelivit. Multi ex paganis per eos ad fidem Christi conversi ac baptizati ibidem requiescunt, quorum, propter eorum multitudinem, non est numerus.” De iisdem eulogii author, libro secundo: “ Ibidem diu commorantes, multisque de dicto regno ad locum confluentibus per eosdem ad Christum conversis, tandem in pace quieverunt. Joseph vero ab Arimathia ibidem sepultus est juxta dictam Ecclesiam [B. Mariæ] cum duabus phialis plenis de sudore Christi sanguineo, quas secum de terra sancta detulerat.” Quis vero inde usque ad Lucii regis tempora loci illius status fuerit, Guilielmus Malmesburienſis his verbis edocet. “Sancti<sup id="fnref-p0067-i"><a href="#fn-seg_p0068_fn_001">i</a></sup> igitur memorati in eadem eremo sic degentes, eﬄuentibus multis annorum curriculis carnis ergastulo sunt educti: idemque locus cœpit esse ferarum latibulum, qui prius fuerat habitatio sanctorum.”

**EN**  That the same man was buried there with his companions I find recorded in the great table of Glastonbury in these words: "There rest twelve disciples, of whom the chief and guardian was Joseph of Arimathea, who buried the Lord. Many of the pagans, converted to the faith of Christ through them and baptized, rest in the same place; of these, on account of their multitude, there is no number." Concerning the same men the author of the "Eulogium" writes in his second book: "Remaining there a long time, while many from the said kingdom flocked to the place and were converted to Christ through them, they at last rested in peace. Joseph of Arimathea, moreover, was buried in that same place beside the said church [of the Blessed Mary], with two vials filled with the bloody sweat of Christ, which he had brought with him from the Holy Land." What the condition of that place was from that time until the days of King Lucius, William of Malmesbury explains in these words: "The saints<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0068_fn_001">i</a></sup> just mentioned, then, living in this manner in that same hermitage, were, after the passing of many courses of years, released from the prison of the flesh; and the same place that had formerly been the habitation of saints began to be a den of wild beasts."
