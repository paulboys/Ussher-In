### `seg_p0039_body_l0002`

### `seg_p0039_body_l0003`

- **Latin:** cæsus occubuit.” Addit Ordericus Vitalis Uticensis mo-
- **v0:** having been struck, he fell." Orderic Vitalis, monk of Saint-Évroul (Uticensis), adds,
- **v2:** he was struck down and died.' Orderic Vitalis, the monk of Saint-Évroul, adds,
- **NOTES:** 
-v0 and v2 both correctly translate the name 'Ordericus Vitalis' into its commonly used form of 'Orderic Vitalis'.  However, both versions take too much liberty in projecting into the translation 'Saint-Évroul' based on outside knowledge of the historical person Orderic Vitalis.  The Latin actually says, 'Orderic Vitalis of Utica, a monk.'  However, Orderic Vitalis seems to have no association with Utica.  So this toponymic epithet seems to be a mistake by the author himself.
