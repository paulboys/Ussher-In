**LA**  Piscium ritu ac locustarum, et velut muscæ et culices con terebantur; absque notitia enim sui Creatoris, omnis homo pecus est. Nunc vero passionem Christi, et resurrectio nem ejus, cunctarum gentium et voces et literæ sonant.”

**EN**  They were being crushed in the manner of fish and locusts, and like flies and gnats; for without knowledge of his Creator, every man is a beast. But now the passion of Christ and his resurrection resound in the voices and writings of all peoples.”

**LA**  Nam quum temporum plenitudo advenisset, “myste rium quod a temporibus seculorum tacitum fuit, factum est manifestum, et per Scripturas propheticas ex imperio æterni Dei ad obedientiam fidei omnibus gentibus decla ratum.” Indeque iis qui in tenebris et umbra mortis erant positi, lumen illud gentibus patefaciendum aﬀulsit: de quo apud prophetam Deus; “Constitui te ut sis lux gentium, ut sis saluti usque ad ﬁnes terræ.” Christo enim apostolis suis imperante, ut profecti in mundum univer sum, prædicarent evangelium omni creaturæ; egressi illi prædicarunt ubique, ita ut nullum locum cœlesti doctrinæ occultum esse sinerent; “μὴ νήσους, μὴ ἤπειρον, μηδ' εἴτινα τρίτην ἀνθρώποις κατοικίαν ἡ φύσις ἐδίδου, non insulas, non continentem, non si quam tertiam habitationem homi nibus natura concessit:” quemadmodum Gregorius Nys senus loquitur. Atque ita completum est quod per Esaiam olim Deus prædixerat: “Mittam ex eis ad gentes in oceano, insulasque remotissimas, quæ non audiverant fa mam meam, neque viderant gloriam meam, ut indicent gloriam meam in gentibus.” Nam “verbum Dei,” Augus tini verba hæc sunt, “non in sola continenti terra præ dicatum est; sed etiam in insulis, quæ constitutæ sunt in medio mari: et ipsæ plenæ Christianis, plenæ sunt ser vis Dei. Non enim separat mare eum, qui fecerat mare.

**EN**  For when the fullness of time had come, “the mystery which had been kept silent from the times of the ages was made manifest, and through the prophetic Scriptures, by the command of the eternal God, was declared to all peoples for the obedience of faith.” And from this that light, destined to be revealed to the peoples, shone upon those who were set in darkness and the shadow of death: concerning which God speaks through the prophet, “I have appointed you to be a light to the peoples, that you may be for salvation to the ends of the earth.” For when Christ commanded his apostles to go out into the whole world and preach the gospel to every creature, they went out and preached everywhere, so that they allowed no place to remain hidden from the heavenly doctrine; “μὴ νήσους, μὴ ἤπειρον, μηδ' εἴτινα τρίτην ἀνθρώποις κατοικίαν ἡ φύσις ἐδίδου [neither the islands, nor the mainland, nor any third dwelling-place that nature had granted to humankind],” as Gregory of Nyssa puts it. And thus was fulfilled what God had once foretold through Isaiah: “I will send some of them to the peoples in the ocean, and to the most remote islands, which had not heard my fame nor seen my glory, that they may declare my glory among the peoples.” For “the word of God,” — these are Augustine’s words — “has been preached not on the continental land alone, but also in the islands, which are set in the midst of the sea: and these too are full of Christians, full of the servants of God. For the sea does not separate them from him who had made the sea.”

## Reading

They were crushed like fish and locusts, like flies and gnats; for without knowledge of his Creator, every man is but a beast. Now, however, the passion of Christ and his resurrection resound in the voices and the writings of all peoples.”

For when the fullness of time had come, “the mystery that had been kept silent through the ages was made manifest, and by the command of the eternal God was declared through the prophetic Scriptures to all peoples, to bring them to the obedience of faith.” From this, that light, destined to be revealed to the nations, dawned upon those who sat in darkness and the shadow of death. Of this God speaks through the prophet: “I have appointed you to be a light to the peoples, that you may bring salvation to the ends of the earth.” For when Christ commanded his apostles to go out into all the world and preach the gospel to every creature, they went out and preached everywhere, leaving no place hidden from the heavenly teaching; they reached, as Gregory of Nyssa says, “μὴ νήσους, μὴ ἤπειρον, μηδ' εἴτινα τρίτην ἀνθρώποις κατοικίαν ἡ φύσις ἐδίδου—neither the islands, nor the mainland, nor any third dwelling-place that nature had granted to humankind.”

And so was fulfilled what God had once foretold through Isaiah: “I will send some of them to the peoples across the ocean, and to the most remote islands, which had not heard my fame nor seen my glory, that they may declare my glory among the nations.” For “the word of God,” in Augustine’s own words, “has been preached not on the continental land alone, but also in the islands set in the midst of the sea; and these too are full of Christians, full of the servants of God. For the sea does not separate them from him who made the sea.”
