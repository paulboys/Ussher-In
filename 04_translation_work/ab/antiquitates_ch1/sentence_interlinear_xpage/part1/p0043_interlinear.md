## Reading

What grace of beauty is hers? Italian mothers could believe her Roman, and Attic mothers one of their own. Thank the gods that, fruitful, she has borne to her devoted husband as many sons-in-law, and as many daughters-in-law, as the girl could hope for. So may it please the gods above that she rejoice in one husband alone, and rejoice always in her three children.

And again, concerning the marriage of Pudens and Claudia: "Claudia, a foreigner, Rufus, weds my Pudens; blessing be upon your torches, O Hymen. So well is rare cinnamon blended with its own nard, so well are Massic wines mingled with Attic honeycombs. No better are elms joined to the tender vines; the lotus loves the waters no more, nor the myrtle the shores. Fair Concord, abide forever upon their couch, and may Venus ever be kind to a yoke so evenly matched. May she love him one day when he is old; and may she, even when she has grown old to her husband, then likewise not seem an old woman."

Although this epigram was written to Rufus about Pudens, Thomas Dempster — a man of much reading but plainly of no judgement — went so far as to assign the name Rufus to Pudens himself, so that from this we might perhaps learn why his wife "Claudia, having taken on the Roman cognomen, was called Rufina." It was with the same acumen, indeed, that he likewise asserts that "Plutarch numbers this same Claudia among the matrons most renowned for the praise of continence" — although Plutarch makes mention of Κλαυδίας τῆς παλαιᾶς, that ancient Claudia, the most celebrated Vestal, of course, "who, against the swelling Albula, drew the vessel well enough with her maiden's hair," and not of this Claudia, who lived in the same age as Pudens. And it is with the like acumen that he gathers, from that poem of Martial, that the husband Pudens — a centurion or primipilus serving in Britain or Germany — was banished as an exile into Scotland, beyond the bounds of the Roman empire, on account of his profession of Christian piety: "While it delights you, Aulus, to behold the Parrhasian oxen at close range, and to bear the stars of the Scythian pole."
