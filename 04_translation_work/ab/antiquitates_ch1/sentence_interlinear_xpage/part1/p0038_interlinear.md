**LA**  Et ita sane in martyrologio Romano (quod Hieronymi<sup id="fnref-p0038-m"><a href="#fn-seg_p0038_fn_004">m</a></sup> nomine olim editum, supposita<sup id="fnref-p0038-n"><a href="#fn-seg_p0038_fn_005">n</a></sup> quoque ejusdem ad Chromatium et Heliodorum epistola, confirmat Baronius) ut et in Bedæ, Usuardi, Notkeri et Adonis martyrologiis, ad Idus Maii, legimus; “ Romæ hos a sanctis apostolis episcopos ordinatos, et ad prædicandum verbum Dei in Hispanias directos esse: cumque diversis urbibus evangelizassent, et innumeras multitudines Christi fidei subjugassent ;” tandem in dictis Hispaniæ locis quievisse. In vetere Romanæ Ecclesiæ martyrologio, quod suo præfixit Ado, non aliud de illis dicitur, quam quod “ Romæ ab apostolis<sup id="fnref-p0038-o"><a href="#fn-seg_p0038_fn_006">o</a></sup> ordinati sunt.” In Britannico vero<sup id="fnref-p0038-p"><a href="#fn-seg_p0038_fn_007">p</a></sup>, quod describendum curavit Ricemarchus Sulgeni Menevensis episcopi filius, ne nominantur quidem. In apostolorum tamen catalogo, qui epistolæ ad Chromatium et Heliodorum ibi subjicitur, de Jacobo legitur: “ Hic Hispaniæ, et in occidentalia loca prædicavit: et sub Herode gladio cæsus occubuit.” Addit Ordericus Vitalis Uticensis monachus, libro secundo ecclesiasticæ historiæ, septem ipsius discipulos cadaver ejus in Hispaniam detulisse, “ et a rege Galliciæ susceptos, primum fidem et religionem Hiberis prædicavisse.” An pro Hiberis Vincentius alicubi Hibernis exaratum repererit, aliis conjiciendum relinquo : certe hujusmodi aliquo errore dictos Jacobi discipulos ad Hiberniam translatos esse, a vero non est absimile.

**EN**  And so indeed in the Roman Martyrology (which Baronius confirms was once published under Jerome's<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0038_fn_004">m</a></sup> name, together with a likewise supposititious<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0038_fn_005">n</a></sup> letter of the same Jerome to Chromatius and Heliodorus), as also in the martyrologies of Bede, Usuard, Notker, and Ado, at the Ides of May, we read that “these men were ordained bishops at Rome by the holy apostles, and were sent into Spain to preach the word of God; and when they had evangelized in various cities, and had brought countless multitudes under the faith of Christ,” they at last rested in the said places of Spain. In the old martyrology of the Roman Church, which Ado prefixed to his own, nothing else is said of them than that “they were ordained at Rome by the apostles.” But in the British martyrology<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0038_fn_007">p</a></sup>, which Ricemarchus, son of Sulgenus bishop of Menevia, saw to be transcribed, they are not even named. Yet in the catalogue of the apostles, which is there appended to the letter to Chromatius and Heliodorus, it is recorded of James: “He preached to Spain and to the western regions, and under Herod he fell, slain by the sword.” Orderic Vitalis, the monk of Ouche, adds, in the second book of his Ecclesiastical History, that seven of his disciples carried his body into Spain, “and, being received by the king of Galicia, first preached the faith and religion to the Iberians.” Whether Vincent somewhere found “Hiberni” (the Irish) written in place of “Hiberi” (the Iberians), I leave for others to conjecture; certainly it is not far from the truth that, through some error of this kind, the said disciples of James were transferred to Ireland.<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0038_fn_006">o</a></sup>

## Reading

But at last, when their preaching had enlightened countless peoples, Torquatus of Acci, Tisephon of Vergium, Secundus of Abula, Indaletius of Urci, Caecilius of Eliberri, Esicius of Carseca, and Eufrasius of Eliturgi died—on the Ides of May.

This is exactly what we read in the Roman Martyrology (which Baronius confirms was once published under Jerome's name, along with an equally spurious letter attributed to Jerome and addressed to Chromatius and Heliodorus), and likewise in the martyrologies of Bede, Usuard, Notker, and Ado, under the Ides of May: that "these men were ordained bishops at Rome by the holy apostles and sent into Spain to preach the word of God; and after they had spread the Gospel through various cities and brought countless multitudes under the faith of Christ," they at last died in those places in Spain already named. In the old martyrology of the Roman Church, which Ado prefixed to his own, nothing further is said of them than that "they were ordained at Rome by the apostles." In the British martyrology, however, which Ricemarchus, son of Sulgenus the bishop of Menevia, arranged to have transcribed, they are not even mentioned.

Yet in the catalogue of the apostles appended there to the letter to Chromatius and Heliodorus, this is recorded of James: "He preached to Spain and to the western regions, and under Herod he fell, slain by the sword." Orderic Vitalis, the monk of Ouche, adds in the second book of his Ecclesiastical History that seven of James's disciples carried his body into Spain, "and, received by the king of Galicia, were the first to preach the faith and religion to the Iberians." Whether Vincent somewhere found "Hiberni"—the Irish—written in place of "Hiberi," the Iberians, I leave for others to conjecture; but it is certainly not far from the truth that, through some error of this kind, these disciples of James came to be transferred to Ireland.
