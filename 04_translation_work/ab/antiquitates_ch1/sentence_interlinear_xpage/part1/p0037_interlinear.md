## Reading

But others show that James was killed by Herod before the apostles were divided into "the various regions of the world" (as Vincent puts it, following Turpin) — namely, the very authorities whom Vincent himself followed. Turpin, in his Life of Charlemagne, and Calixtus II, in his book on the miracles of St. James the Apostle and the translation of his body (if indeed the two works were not by one and the same author), report that these events took place not in Ireland but in Galicia.

"While the apostle was still living," says Calixtus in his preface, "he is said to have chosen nine disciples in Galicia. Seven of them — the other two staying behind in Galicia to preach — went with him to Jerusalem, and after his martyrdom carried his body by sea to Galicia. Of these the blessed Jerome spoke as follows in his martyrology, and so wrote to the blessed Chromatius: that, once the body of blessed James had been buried in Galicia, they were ordained at Rome by the apostles Peter and Paul with the episcopal mitre, and were sent to preach the word of God to the Spains, which were still entangled in pagan error."
