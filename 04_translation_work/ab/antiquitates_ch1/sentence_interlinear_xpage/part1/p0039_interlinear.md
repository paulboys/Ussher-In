## Reading

Simon the Zealot carried the teaching of the Gospel "to the western ocean and the British Isles," as we have on the authority of Nicephorus Callistus; and Dorotheus, in his synopsis, records that the same man "was crucified, slain, and buried in Britain," with the Greek menologies likewise concurring. In them, under the tenth day of May, the following is written concerning Simon: Ὕστερον δὲ ἐν Βρετανίᾳ γενόμενος, καὶ πολλοὺς τῷ λόγῳ τοῦ εὐαγγελίου φωτίσας, ὑπὸ τῶν ἀπίστων σταυρωθεὶς καὶ τεθεὶς θάπτεται ἐκεῖ [Afterward, having come into Britain and enlightened many by the word of the Gospel, he was crucified by the unbelievers and, being laid in the tomb, is buried there]. Yet in the Roman Martyrology and Breviary, as also in the martyrologies of Bede, Usuard, and Ado, he is recorded under the twenty-eighth day of October as having undergone martyrdom in Persia.

Eusebius Pamphili reports somewhere that Simon Peter "spent twelve years in the East, but passed twenty-three years at Rome, and in Britain, and in the cities which lie in the West"—if indeed Simeon Metaphrastes is to be believed. For in Metaphrastes I also read that Peter "remained a long time in Britain, and drew many unnamed peoples to the faith of Christ," but that at the last, when he had "enlightened many by the word of grace, established churches, and ordained bishops, presbyters, and deacons, he returned again to Rome in the twelfth year of Caesar Nero."
