**LA**  Ubi argutissimus criticus Scotici vocabulum, pro Scythici, reponendum esse conjicit: oblitus, apud Claudianum, Apollinarem Sidonium ac veteres illos, primam Scotici nominis syllabam semper productam legi; et in antiquis manuscriptis Scottos geminata litera passim exarari consuevisse. Neque sui dissimilis est, quum addit, Personium “probare<sup id="fnref-p0044-p"><a href="#fn-seg_p0044_fn_002">p</a></sup> in conversione Anglicana Claudiam non potuisse esse Anglam,” quasi quisquam eam Dempsteri insaniret insaniam, ut Claudiæ temporibus Angliam quæreret in Britannia: aut id argumentatione sua conficere non conaretur Personius, B. Claudiam cujus Paulus meminit, non fuisse Britannam illam, cujus formæ decus Trajano imperante Martialis laudaverit; et consequenter, absque ullo fundamento vel Scotam constitui vel Anglam. Quanquam argumentum illud, quo potissimum ille nititur, a temporis ratione deductum, non multum urgeat. Neque enim si in undecimo quem citavimus epigrammatum libello occurrrunt aliqua sub<sup id="fnref-p0044-q"><a href="#fn-seg_p0044_fn_003">q</a></sup> tertio vel Nervæ vel Trajani consulatu conscripta carmina; sequetur, et reliqua omnia eodem tempore fuisse composita. Et licet sub Trajano mortem poeta obierit: senem tamen decessisse cogitandum est, et prius familiariter usum esse Silio Italico, secundi belli Punici scriptore: quo consulatum gerente, Neronem interiisse, et alii authores confirmant, et ipse Martialis<sup id="fnref-p0044-r">r</sup> significat illis de Silio versiculis : Postquam bis senis ingentem fascibus annum Rexerat, asserto qui sacer orbe fuit : Emeritos Musis et Phœbo tradidit annos, Proque suo celebrat nunc Helicona foro.

**EN**  Here the most ingenious critic conjectures that the word "Scotici" should be substituted for "Scythici" — forgetting that in Claudian, Apollinaris Sidonius, and those ancient writers the first syllable of the name "Scoti" is always read long, and that in old manuscripts "Scotti" was regularly written throughout with a doubled letter. And he is quite in character when he adds that Persons "proves, in his "Conversion of England," that Claudia could not have been an Englishwoman" — as though anyone would rave with Dempster's madness and search for England in Britain in Claudia's day, or as though Persons were not trying to establish by his argument that the blessed Claudia whom Paul mentions was not that British woman whose beauty Martial praised under the emperor Trajan, and was consequently set up, without any foundation, as either a Scotswoman or an Englishwoman. And yet that argument on which he chiefly relies, drawn from chronological reckoning, carries little weight. For even if, in the eleventh book of epigrams which I cited, there occur certain poems composed under the third consulship of either Nerva or Trajan, it does not follow that all the rest were composed at the same time. And although the poet died under Trajan, it must be supposed that he died an old man, and that he had earlier been on familiar terms with Silius Italicus, the writer on the Second Punic War — during whose consulship Nero perished, as both other authorities confirm and Martial himself indicates in those verses about Silius: "After he had governed the mighty year with its twice-six fasces — he who was hallowed when the world had been set free — he gave his retirement years to the Muses and to Phoebus, and now frequents Helicon in place of his own forum."<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0044_fn_002">p</a></sup><sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0044_fn_003">q</a></sup><sup>r</sup>

## Reading

Here the most ingenious critic conjectures that the word "Scotici" should be substituted for "Scythici" — forgetting that in Claudian, Apollinaris Sidonius, and those ancient writers the first syllable of the name "Scoti" is always scanned long, and that in old manuscripts "Scotti" was regularly written throughout with a doubled letter. And he is quite in character when he adds that Persons "proves, in his Conversion of England, that Claudia could not have been an Englishwoman" — as though anyone would share Dempster's madness and go looking for England in the Britain of Claudia's day, or as though Persons were not trying to establish by his argument that the blessed Claudia whom Paul mentions was not that British woman whose beauty Martial praised under the emperor Trajan, and was consequently set up, with no foundation whatever, as either a Scotswoman or an Englishwoman.

And yet the argument on which he chiefly relies, drawn from chronological reckoning, carries little weight. For even if certain poems composed under the third consulship of either Nerva or Trajan occur in the eleventh book of epigrams that I cited, it does not follow that all the rest were composed at the same time. And although the poet died under Trajan, we must suppose that he died an old man, and that he had earlier been on familiar terms with Silius Italicus, the author who wrote on the Second Punic War. During the consulship of Silius, Nero perished — as both other authorities confirm and Martial himself indicates in those verses about him: "After he had governed the mighty year with its twice-six fasces — he who was hallowed when the world had been set free — he gave his retirement years to the Muses and to Phoebus, and now frequents Helicon in place of his own forum."
