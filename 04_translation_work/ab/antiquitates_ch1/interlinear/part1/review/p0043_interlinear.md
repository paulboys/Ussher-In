## Reading

...the Pontifical Book asserts. Our own writers hold that Claudia was British by birth and the wife of Pudens, as they gather from the poet Martial, who<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0043_fn_001">i</a></sup> sang of Claudia in these words:

Though Claudia Rufina is sprung from the woad-blue Britons, why does she hold the heart of the Latin people? What grace of form is hers! Italian mothers could believe her Roman, and the women of Attica could believe her one of their own. The gods do well, that, fruitful, she has borne to her holy husband as many sons-in-law as she hopes for, and as many daughters-in-law, while still a girl. So may it please the gods above that she rejoice in one husband alone, and rejoice forever in her three children.

And again, on the marriage<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0043_fn_005">k</a></sup> of Pudens and Claudia:

Claudia, Rufus, the foreign woman, weds my Pudens; be honored, Hymen, with your torches. So well is rare cinnamon blended with its nard, so well are Massic wines with Attic honeycombs. Nor are elms more happily joined to the tender vines, nor does the lotus love the waters more, or the myrtle the shores. Fair Concord, abide upon their everlasting bed, and may Venus ever be impartial to a yoke so evenly matched. May she love him one day even when he is old; but may she too, when she has herself grown old, then not seem an old woman to her husband.

Although this epigram was written to Rufus about Pudens, Thomas Dempster,<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0043_fn_002">l</a></sup> a man of much reading but plainly of no judgment, attached the name Rufus to Pudens himself as well, so that we might perhaps learn from it why his wife was called "Claudia Rufina, having taken on a Roman surname"—with the same acumen, namely, by which the same man asserts that this same Claudia "is numbered by Plutarch<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0043_fn_003">m</a></sup> among the matrons most renowned for the praise of continence"; since Plutarch, speaking of Κλαυδίας τῆς παλαιᾶς, that ancient Claudia—the most celebrated Vestal Virgin, of course,

Who,<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0043_fn_004">n</a></sup> against the Albula swelling high,
drew the vessel by her maiden's hair—

makes no mention of this Claudia, who lived in the same age as he did.
