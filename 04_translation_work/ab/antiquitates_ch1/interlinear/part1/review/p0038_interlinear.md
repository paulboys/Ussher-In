## Reading

report that these events took place in Galicia. "Nine disciples," says Calixtus<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0038_fn_001">h</a></sup> in his preface, "are said to have been chosen in Galicia by the apostle while he was still living. Seven of them, leaving the other two behind in Galicia to preach, accompanied him to Jerusalem, and after his passion carried his body by sea to Galicia. Concerning these men, blessed Jerome spoke as follows in his martyrology and wrote to blessed Chromatius: that, once the body of blessed James had been buried in Galicia, they were ordained at Rome by the apostles Peter and Paul with the episcopal insignia and were sent to preach the word of God into the Spanish provinces, which were still entangled in pagan error. At length, when countless peoples had been enlightened by their preaching, Torquatus of Acci, Tisephon<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0038_fn_002">k</a></sup> of Vergium, Secundus of Abula, Indaletius of Urci, Caecilius of Eliberris, Esicius<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0038_fn_003">l</a></sup> of Carseca, and Eufrasius of Eliturgi found their rest, namely on the Ides of May."

And so indeed we read in the Roman martyrology (which Baronius<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0038_fn_004">m</a></sup> confirms was once published under the name of Jerome, the epistle<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0038_fn_005">n</a></sup> of the same Jerome to Chromatius and Heliodorus being likewise spurious), and also in the martyrologies of Bede, Usuard, Notker, and Ado, at the Ides of May: that "these men were ordained bishops at Rome by the holy apostles and were sent to preach the word of God into the Spanish provinces, and that, after they had evangelized in various cities and brought countless multitudes into subjection to the faith of Christ," they at last found their rest in the said places of Spain. In the old martyrology of the Roman Church, which Ado prefixed to his own, nothing more is said of them than that "at Rome they were ordained by the apostles.<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0038_fn_006">o</a></sup>" But in the British martyrology<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0038_fn_007">p</a></sup>, which Ricemarchus, son of Sulgenus, bishop of Menevia, took care to transcribe, they are not even named.
