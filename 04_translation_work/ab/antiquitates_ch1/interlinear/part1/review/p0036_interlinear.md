## Reading

Hence Arnobius<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0036_fn_001">y</a></sup> writes: "So swiftly does his word run that, although for so many thousands of years God was known in Judaea alone, now within a few years he lies hidden neither from the Indians in the East nor from the Britons in the West. Everywhere his word has run swiftly."

Gildas, however, places the first dawning of Christ's truth upon Britain between two moments: the final period of Tiberius Caesar, when the light of the Gospel began to spread throughout the whole world, and the campaign waged by the Romans against the Britons—of which he had just been speaking, after the Romans had suffered that very great disaster at the hands of Queen Boudica, whom Gildas calls treacherous. That is, he indicates that Christ first granted the rays of his truth to Britain between the 36th year of our common Christian era and the 61st, the year in which the province was recovered once more by Suetonius Paulinus, with eighty thousand of the Britons slain.

Hence Theodoret too, in the ninth book of his Cure of the Greek Maladies, counts the Britons among the peoples converted to the faith through the ministry of the apostles. "Our fishermen and tax-collectors," he says, "and that shoemaker of ours, brought the teachings of the Gospel to all nations: not only the Romans and those who live under Roman rule, but also the Scythians and Sauromatians, and besides them the Indians, the Ethiopians, the Persians, the Seres, the Hyrcanians, the Britons, the Cimmerians, and the Germans—and, to say it in a word, every race of men and all nations—they persuaded to accept the teachings of the Crucified."

But which member of that company of the apostles came to our British isles is a point on which the authorities do not agree.
