## Reading

I report on the authority of others, for I have not yet had occasion to see the author for myself. An old translator of Chrysostom's sermon on<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0041_fn_001">c</a></sup> Pentecost also intimates that he ran from Spain "into the farthest parts" of the earth. Finally, "like some heavenly<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0041_fn_002">d</a></sup> bird traversing the inhabited world round about, with the gospel contest for Christ accomplished over thirty-five<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0041_fn_003">e</a></sup> years"—as Nicephorus writes—he at last completed his course by martyrdom at Rome under Nero.

Aristobulus too, whom Paul himself mentions in the epistle to the Romans<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0041_fn_004">f</a></sup>, I find recorded in the Menaea of the Greeks as a bishop of the Britons ordained by Paul; for under the fifteenth of March a commemoration of him is made in these terms: "This man was one of the seventy disciples, and he followed the holy apostle Paul, preaching the gospel throughout the whole inhabited world and ministering to him; by whom he was also ordained bishop to the land of the Britons, savage and most cruel men, by whom—now beaten, now dragged through the marketplace—he persuaded many to come to Christ; whence also, having established churches and appointed presbyters and deacons in them, he was made perfect."
