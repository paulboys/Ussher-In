Ordericus Vitalis, the monk of Saint-Évroul, adds in the second book of his Ecclesiastical History that seven of James's disciples carried his body into Spain, "and, having been received by the king of Galicia, were the first to preach the faith and religion to the Iberians." Whether Vincent somewhere found "Hibernis" (the Irish) written instead of "Hiberis," I leave for others to conjecture; certainly it is not improbable that the disciples of James were transferred to Ireland through some such error.

That Simon Zelotes carried the doctrine of the Gospel "to the western ocean and the British isles" is attested by Nicephorus<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0039_fn_001">q</a></sup> Callistus. That the same Simon "was crucified, slain, and buried in Britain" is recorded by Dorotheus in his synopsis, with the Greek menologies also in agreement<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0039_fn_002">r</a></sup>. In these, on the tenth day of May, we read concerning Simon as follows: Ὕστερον δὲ ἐν Βρετανίᾳ γενόμενος, καὶ πολλοὺς τῷ λόγῳ τοῦ εὐαγγελίου φωτίσας, ὑπὸ τῶν ἀπίστων σταυρωθεὶς καὶ τεθεὶς θάπτεται ἐκεῖ. That is, having afterward come to Britain and enlightened many by the word of the Gospel, he was crucified by the unbelievers and buried there. In the Roman martyrology, however, and in the Roman breviary, as also in the martyrologies of Bede, Usuard, and Ado, he is recorded to have undergone martyrdom in Persia on the twenty-eighth day of October.

That Simon Peter "spent twelve years in the East, but passed twenty-three years at Rome, in Britain, and in the cities which are in the West," Eusebius of Caesarea reported somewhere—if indeed we are to believe Simeon Metaphrastes.
