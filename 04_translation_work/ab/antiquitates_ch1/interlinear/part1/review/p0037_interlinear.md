## Reading

Some report<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0037_fn_001">z</a></sup> that the same man wrote in Arabic on the martyrs. Isidore likewise records, in his book On the Fathers of Both Testaments,<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0037_fn_002">a</a></sup> and after him Freculph of Lisieux,<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0037_fn_003">b</a></sup> that this same James "preached the gospel to the peoples of Spain and the western regions, and poured the light of preaching into the setting sun of the world." The author of the chronicle that circulates under the name of Maximus, bishop of Saragossa, likewise asserts<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0037_fn_004">c</a></sup> that he was "the first of all the apostles to preach in the western regions." That James preached to the Britons is affirmed by the writer who dons the mask of Flavius Lucius Dexter; that he preached to the Irish is affirmed by Vincent of Beauvais.

The former, in his chronicle, under the year of Christ 41, writes: "Returning, James visited Gaul, Britain, and the towns of the Veneti<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0037_fn_005">d</a></sup>, where he preached; and he returned to Jerusalem to consult the blessed Virgin and Peter on matters of the gravest weight." The latter writes thus in the Historical Mirror<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0037_fn_006">e</a></sup> (a passage also produced at the Council of Basel by Alfonso García de Santa María, ambassador of the king of Castile): "As the apostles were going to the various regions of the world, James, driven by God's will to the shores of Ireland, fearlessly preached the word of God, where he is said to have chosen seven disciples: namely Torquatus, Secundus, Indaletius, Tisefon, Euphrasius, Cecilius, and Hesychius, through whose company he might root out the darnel and entrust the seeds of the word of God to soil that was dry and long barren. And when his final day drew near, he went up to Jerusalem with them, and there underwent martyrdom."

But that James was killed by Herod before the apostles were dispersed into "the various regions of the world" (to use Vincent's phrase, borrowed from Turpin<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0037_fn_007">f</a></sup>) is shown by others<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0037_fn_008">g</a></sup> — indeed by the very authorities whom Vincent himself followed.
