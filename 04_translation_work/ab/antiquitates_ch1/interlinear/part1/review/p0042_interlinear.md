Similarly, in the Synopsis of Dorotheus we read that Aristobulus was made bishop of Britain; and in a fragment published under the name of Heleca, bishop of Caesaraugusta: "Among the Britons the memory of many martyrs is celebrated, especially of St. Aristobulus, one of the seventy-two disciples, who was also called Zebedee (father of James and of John the martyr, and of Mary Salome), who went to Rome with Peter. From there, leaving his household behind, he was sent into England as bishop, and fell as a martyr in the second year of the emperor Nero, that most savage man." So it stands on page 108 of the Caesaraugustan edition. But what I have enclosed in parenthesis should be corrected to read: "Father of James and John, husband of Mary Salome." For on the following page Concordia is reported to have been the wife of the apostle Peter and the daughter of Aristobulus and Salome. "St. Peter," the author says, "is held in honor in the Spains, and his wife Concordia suffered at Rome under Nero, not long before he himself suffered. She was, as M. Maximus teaches in his commentaries on the epistle of St. Peter, the daughter of Aristobulus (or Zebedee) and of Salome; and also the mother-in-law of Andrew, who married another sister of Concordia, and of James and John. Now Aristobulus, surnamed Zebedee, was the brother of Barnabas, the apostle of the Lord." Thus that author; and with him, on the point that Peter took as wife the daughter of Aristobulus, the brother of the apostle Barnabas, the Greeks too agree, in Metaphrastes, in his commentary on Peter and Paul, and in the Menaia, at June 29.

At the end of the second epistle to Timothy,<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0042_fn_001">g</a></sup> the apostle commemorates together Pudens, Linus, and Claudia. Pseudo-Clement intimates that Linus, the first bishop of the Roman Church, ordained by Paul, was the son of Claudia.<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0042_fn_002">h</a></sup> But the Liber Pontificalis asserts that the father of Linus was a certain Herculanus.
