## Reading

...of the eternal God, made known to all the nations for the obedience of faith. And so upon those who lay in darkness and the shadow of death there shone forth that light which was to be revealed to the nations—the light of which God speaks through the prophet: "I have appointed<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0034_fn_001">l</a></sup> you to be a light of the nations, that you may be for salvation to the ends of the earth." For when Christ commanded his apostles that,<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0034_fn_002">m</a></sup> having gone out into the whole world, they should preach the gospel to every creature, they went out and preached everywhere, allowing no place to remain hidden from the heavenly teaching: "neither islands, nor mainland, nor any third dwelling-place that nature granted to men," as Gregory<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0034_fn_003">n</a></sup> of Nyssa puts it. And so was fulfilled what God had long before foretold through Isaiah: "I will send<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0034_fn_004">o</a></sup> from among them to the nations in the ocean, and to the most remote islands, which had not heard my fame nor seen my glory, that they may declare my glory among the nations." For "the word of God"—these are Augustine's words<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0034_fn_005">p</a></sup>—"was preached not on the mainland alone, but also on the islands set in the midst of the sea; and these too are full of Christians, full of the servants of God. For the sea does not separate him who made the sea. Where ships can reach, can the words of God not reach?"

And indeed, that Britain received the Gospel from its very first rising... to the island stiff with glacial cold and, as if by a longer withdrawal of its lands, set far from the visible sun, that true Sun—shining forth to the whole world and displaying the radiant gleam of himself not from the temporal firmament alone<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0034_fn_006">q</a></sup>, but also from the highest citadel of the heavens that surpasses all ages—first grants his rays, as we know, in the last period of Tiberius Caesar (during which his religion was being propagated without any hindrance, since death was threatened by the prince<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0034_fn_007">r</a></sup> himself, against the wishes of the senate, upon the informers<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0034_fn_008">s</a></sup> who denounced his soldiers): first grants his rays, that is to say, Christ grants his precepts.
