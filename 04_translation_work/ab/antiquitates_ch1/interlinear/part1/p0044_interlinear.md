While it delights you, Aulus, to gaze<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0044_fn_001">o</a></sup> upon the Parrhasian Wains close at hand, and to bear the stars of the Scythian pole.

Here this most ingenious critic conjectures that "Scotici" should be substituted for "Scythici" — forgetting that in Claudian, in Apollinaris Sidonius, and among the ancient writers generally, the first syllable of "Scoti-" is always scanned as long, and that in old manuscripts "Scottos" was customarily written everywhere with a doubled letter.

Nor is he inconsistent with himself when he adds that Persons "proves<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0044_fn_002">p</a></sup> in his Conversion of England that Claudia could not have been an Englishwoman" — as though anyone would rave with the madness of Dempster so as to look for England in Britain in the days of Claudia, or as though Persons were not in fact arguing that the blessed Claudia whom Paul mentions was not that British woman whose beauty Martial praised during Trajan's reign, and that she is therefore set down, without any foundation, as either a Scotswoman or an Englishwoman.

That argument, however, on which he chiefly relies, drawn from chronological reckoning, does not press very hard. For if in the eleventh book of epigrams that I cited there occur certain poems composed under<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0044_fn_003">q</a></sup> the third consulship of either Nerva or Trajan, it does not follow that all the rest were composed at the same time.
