## Reading

...from the Hebrews<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0033_fn_001">e</a></sup> attempted to derive the origin of the Irish. For even Origen,<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0033_fn_002">f</a></sup> whom they cite as their authority, plainly indicates the contrary — namely, that "the land of Britain, before the coming of Christ, had by no means assented to the religion of one God." More specifically, Dio Cassius<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0033_fn_003">g</a></sup> records that Andraste, or Andate, the goddess of Victory, was customarily worshipped by the Britons, and others have recorded the same of Apollo and Diana. Concerning their idolatrous frenzy, their own countryman Gildas gives so clear a testimony that the matter ought not be called into question. For he says: "Therefore, passing over those ancient errors shared with all peoples, by which the whole human race was held fast in bondage before the coming of Christ in the flesh; not enumerating the very diabolical prodigies of my homeland, almost surpassing in number those of Egypt — some of which we still behold, with features still misshapen, within or outside the deserted walls, rigid in their customary fashion and with grim countenances; nor crying out by name against the mountains themselves, or the hills, or the rivers, once deadly but now useful for human purposes, on which divine honour was then heaped by a blind people, and so forth."

So that, concerning the most wretched condition of those times and the most happy condition of those that followed, the blessed Jerome did not hesitate to declare in the epitaph of Nepotian: "Where<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0033_fn_004">h</a></sup> then were the men of the whole world, from India as far as Britain, from the frozen region of the north as far as the heats of the Atlantic Ocean — so countless peoples, and the multitudes of so many nations; how varied in their tongues, as in the fashion of their dress and in their arms!<sup><a href="#fn-seg_p0033_fn_005">i</a></sup> After the manner of fish and locusts, and like flies and gnats, they were ground down; for without knowledge of his Creator, every man is a beast. But now the voices and writings of all nations proclaim the passion of Christ and his resurrection."
