Whether Vincent somewhere found "Hibernis" (the Irish) written in place of "Hiberis," I leave to others to conjecture; certainly it is not far from the truth that, through some error of this kind, James's disciples were said to have been brought to Ireland.

Nicephorus Callistus is our authority that Simon Zelotes carried the doctrine of the Gospel "to the western ocean and the British isles." Dorotheus, in his synopsis, relates that this same Simon "was crucified, slain, and buried in Britain," and the Greek menologies agree with him.

In these menologies, under the tenth of May, we read the following written about Simon: Ὕστερον δὲ ἐν Βρετανίᾳ γενόμενος, καὶ πολλοὺς τῷ λόγῳ τοῦ εὐαγγελίου φωτίσας, ὑπὸ τῶν ἀπίστων σταυρωθεὶς καὶ τεθεὶς θάπτεται ἐκεῖ—that is, Afterwards, having gone to Britain and enlightened many by the word of the Gospel, he was crucified by the unbelievers and, having been laid to rest, is buried there. In the Roman martyrology and breviary, however, as also in the martyrologies of Bede, Usuard, and Ado, he is recorded to have suffered martyrdom in Persia on the twenty-eighth of October.
