## Reading

They report that these things took place in Galicia. "The apostle," says Calixtus in his preface, "is said to have chosen nine disciples in Galicia while he was still living. Of these, seven went with him to Jerusalem—the other two staying behind in Galicia to preach—and after his martyrdom they carried his body by sea back to Galicia. Concerning them the blessed Jerome wrote as follows in his martyrology, and in his letter to the blessed Chromatius: that once the body of the blessed James had been buried in Galicia, they were ordained at Rome with episcopal insignia by the apostles Peter and Paul, and were sent to preach the word of God to Spain, which was still entangled in pagan error. At last, after they had enlightened countless peoples by their preaching, they came to their rest—Torquatus at Acci, Tisephon at Vergium, Secundus at Abula, Indalecius at Urci, Cæcilius at Eliberri, Esicius at Carseca, and Eufrasius at Eliturgi—on the Ides of May."

And indeed we read exactly this in the Roman martyrology—which Baronius confirms was once published under Jerome's name, together with a spurious letter of the same Jerome to Chromatius and Heliodorus—as also in the martyrologies of Bede, Usuard, Notker, and Ado, under the Ides of May: that "at Rome these men were ordained bishops by the holy apostles, and sent to preach the word of God into Spain; and when they had evangelized various cities and brought countless multitudes under the faith of Christ," they at last came to their rest in the said places of Spain. In the ancient martyrology of the Roman Church, which Ado prefixed to his own, nothing more is said of them than that "at Rome they were ordained by the apostles." But in the British martyrology, which Ricemarchus, son of Sulgenus the bishop of Menevia, saw to the copying of, they are not even named.
