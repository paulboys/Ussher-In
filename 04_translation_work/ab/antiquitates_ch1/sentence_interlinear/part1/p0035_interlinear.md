The words of Tertullian are these: "Tiberius, in whose time the Christian name entered the world, when word was brought to him from Syria Palestine that the truth of that divinity had been revealed there, laid the matter before the senate with the prerogative of his own vote in its favor. The senate, because it had not itself approved it, rejected it. Caesar held to his opinion, threatening danger to those who accused the Christians."

To this Eusebius adds, in the second book of the *Ecclesiastical History*, chapter three, in the translation of Rufinus: "This, surely, divine providence at that time so impressed upon Caesar's mind that, with no obstacle whatever, in its very first beginnings the word of the Gospel might run everywhere on every side. From this it also came about that suddenly, as though a light had been shown from heaven, or a ray of the sun had burst forth, the brightness of that heavenly light illumined the whole world — so that the prophecy was fulfilled which had said, *Their sound went out into all the earth* (namely, the sound of the evangelists and apostles) *and their words to the ends of the world*. From this time, throughout all cities and villages, immense multitudes of people gathered to the Churches, as grain is gathered to the threshing floors at harvest time." "The congregation of the Christians made its way into every kind of mankind; nor did any nation of the Roman world remain that was left without a share in his worship."
