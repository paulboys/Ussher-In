Here , indeed , when in the 30th session this
question was moved "whether it accords with
right and reason to put on an equality the king-
dom of England and the kingdom of France in
General Council " : on behalf of the authority of
the English Church it was , among other things,
alleged that "Immediately after the Passion of
Christ, Joseph of Arimathea , a noble Decurion ,
who took down Christ from the Cross , with
xii companions immediately proceeded to culti-
vate the Lord's vineyard , that is to say England ,
and converted the peoples to the faith . To whom
the king assigned xii hides of land in the Diocese
of Bath for their maintenance : who , so it is
written, are buried in the Monastery of Glaston
in the diocese of Bath , concerning which xii
hides aforementioned the said Monastery is dis-
tinguished as having been endowed from ancient
times . But the kingdom of France received the
faith of Christ in the time of Saint Dionysius and
by his ministry."
The same claim also was propounded in the
Council of Siena in the year 1424 by Richard
Fleming, Bishop of Lincoln and founder of
Lincoln College , Oxford , when this contro-
versy was renewed by the English against the
Spanish , Scots and French before Martin V.
s (translated ) . Very nearly the same things may be read in the
Book concerning the provinces of England , MS . in the Cottonian
Library .
